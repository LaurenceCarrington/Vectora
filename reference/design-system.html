Thin 20px charcoal millimetre rulers sit along the canvas’s left edge, immediately inside the tool rail, and along the bottom above the status bar. Tick positions share the drawing’s document origin and scale: X increases rightwards, Y downwards, including negative coordinates. Panning, zooming, resizing and zoom reset update both rulers. Labels use readable 1/2/5 intervals with smaller subdivisions; this changes label density only, never the configured grid size. Rulers remain below menus and docked panels, and are display-only guides excluded from exports. The mm corner identifies both scales.

The 28px charcoal status bar spans the full window width at the bottom. Side rails and docked panels end above it. The tool label stays left, the zoom/grid readout stays exactly centred using equal flexible side columns, and the navigation hint stays right (hidden on narrow screens). Click the zoom percentage in the centred footer to reset the view to 100%, or focus it and press Enter or Space. Reset keeps the current canvas centre, artwork and selection, without changing grid spacing or adding an undo step.

Select (V): drag from empty canvas to enclose multiple shapes; fully enclosed, visible and unlocked shapes are selected. Shift-click toggles a shape; Shift-drag adds enclosed shapes. A blue dashed marquee with a translucent blue fill previews the selection. Each selected shape has a dashed outline, with shared bounds and resize handles. Move, resize, numeric bounds edits and Delete apply to the whole selection in one undo step; Shift preserves proportions. Escape cancels a drag or clears selection. Creating or selecting shapes does not open it; an already open panel stays open and updates to the selection. Menus and floating panels share the toolbar’s #2A2B2E charcoal surface, white text, light-grey secondary labels, subtle grey dividers, and dark selected rows with blue icons. Layer transfers: drag objects from expanded layers onto another visible, unlocked layer. Dropping onto Cut Path applies the shared red stroke without changing geometry, and supports Undo/Redo. Cut paths export without enabling artwork export. Layers: fixed header, layer count, 52px selectable rows, pastel markers, expanders, visibility and lock buttons, plus the standard footer. The editor starts with Cut Path, Engrave Path, Construction Path and Artwork; adding and deleting layers is supported. Visibility affects only the canvas, not DXF export. Footer: independent left and right sections, with zoom/grid status fixed at the exact horizontal centre. The empty canvas shows only the grid, with no welcome or starting text. Edge-to-edge grid with no canvas padding; the full-width 48px application header shows the Vectora mark at left and the document name centred. The bars meet without overlap at every viewport size. Footer labels sit inside the side rails. Toolbar controls retain their internal spacing; the footer has its own 24px inset. Grid: dark #202226 background with subtle 1px minor lines and stronger major lines every five cells. Three ways to draw an arc

Centre arc (A): drag from the centre to create a semicircle and adjust it with circular handles. Three-point arc (Shift+A): click the start, a point on the curve, and the end. All three points snap in document millimetres. Backspace removes the previous point; Escape, switching tools or focus loss cancels. Invalid repeated or collinear points keep the draft for correction. Three-point arcs retain the original bounds-based editing; use Select to move or resize them. Centre arc (A): drag from the centre to set the radius and orientation. Release to create an exact 180° semicircle and switch to Select, ready to adjust its handles. Centre and radius snap in document millimetres. Rotation and sweep magnet to 90° increments within 4°; Shift locks 15° increments. Escape, tool switching or focus loss cancels an unfinished drag. Properties stays closed unless opened explicitly.

The centre handle moves the arc; the start square rotates it without changing the sweep; the end square changes the sweep around its fixed centre; the diamond changes radius. Arc handles preserve a circular curve. Radius, Start and Sweep fields accept exact values, with a Semicircle 180° preset and Flip arc action. Each edit is one undo step. Arcs remain open and unfilled, support red Cut Path transfer and open DXF export, and cannot produce a closed sticker outline. Moving, uniform resizing and undo retain arc controls. Nonuniform resizing of a multi-selection converts affected arcs into freeform curves with ordinary bounds handles.

Delete tools

Dissect delete · K / Line delete · Shift+K
Delete

Choose a delete tool

Preview before removing

The eraser icon below Arcs opens the same charcoal pop-out used by the other drawing tools. Hover highlights the exact deletion in red using a 5px stroke at 85% opacity; click commits one undo step. Escape returns to Select. The highlight never exports. Properties stays closed unless explicitly opened.

Dissect delete removes the clicked section between existing path points or intersections. Rectangle edges, polygon sides, polyline segments and curved sections can be removed without crossing lines. Closed outlines open at the deleted section; surviving connected sections stay connected. A single unbroken line or curve can be removed in full. Empty clicks do nothing. Visible paths, including locked reference paths, provide boundaries; hidden paths do not. Bézier curves are split without flattening, and grid snapping does not move the cuts. Remaining sections stay in their original layer and retain their stroke style. Trimmed shapes become open paths with ordinary editing handles.

Line delete removes the entire clicked line, polyline, arc, or shape outline (including all contours of a compound object). Both tools target strokes only, affect one object per click, work on Artwork and Cut Path, and never alter hidden or locked objects. Delete/Backspace retains its existing selected-object behavior.

Line · L / Polyline · P / Freehand · F
Lines
Line tools ready.

Open paths, precise points.

Every drawing-tool pop-out sits flush against the rail, aligned with its own trigger. Near the bottom of the window it shifts up only enough to stay visible; oversized menus scroll internally. Position updates when the window resizes. The line icon sits directly above Shapes on the left rail. Its charcoal pop-out contains Line, Polyline and Freehand. Line (L): drag between endpoints. Polyline (P): click successive points, then Enter or double-click to finish. Backspace removes the last point; Escape cancels the unfinished path. Both snap endpoints in document millimetres; Shift constrains a segment to 45° steps and takes priority over snapping.

Open paths have no fill and select by their stroke. Two-point paths use endpoint handles. Horizontal and vertical paths keep their zero dimension; use endpoints to change direction. Freehand (F): press and drag with a mouse, pen or touch, then release to finish one open, unfilled stroke. The live preview follows the pointer; light smoothing on release removes jitter while preserving endpoints. Freehand drawing ignores grid snapping and Shift constraints so the stroke follows your hand; later moves and resizes still use normal snapping. Artwork defaults to white, Cut Path stays red, and each completed stroke is one undo step. A click without dragging makes no object. Escape, tool switching, pointer cancellation and focus loss discard an unfinished stroke. Properties does not auto-open. Freehand paths support selection, resizing, cut-layer transfer, both delete tools and open DXF export; they never auto-close.

Object snapping

Exact geometry · 10px capture radius

Open Preferences → Snapping to enable Intersection, Nearest, Centre, Tangent or Perpendicular independently (off by default). Exact document-space geometry takes priority over grid rounding within 10 screen pixels; discrete points take priority over Nearest. Visible locked objects remain valid references; hidden objects and the objects being transformed are excluded. Tangent works on curves from a line’s start point or a polyline’s previous vertex; Perpendicular projects that point onto an existing straight segment. Centres include circular arcs and rotated ellipses. Works for drawing, movement and corner resizing; Shift constraints take priority, freehand and numeric input remain exact. The blue marker and label are temporary and never export.

Aligned dimension · D
Dimensions & callouts

The left toolbar has a dedicated Dimensions & callouts icon before Delete. Its dark pop-out contains Aligned dimension (D), Linear dimension, Radial dimension, Diameter dimension and Leader callout. Dissect delete now uses K; Line delete uses Shift+K.

Aligned and linear: click two reference points, then click to place the dimension line. Aligned follows the point-to-point direction. Linear measures the horizontal or vertical distance; move above/below for horizontal, or to either side for vertical. Radial and diameter: click a circle or circular arc, then position the label. Readouts use R or Ø and real document millimetres, rounded to two decimals. Ellipses have no single radius and are not accepted.

Leader callout: click the arrow tip, elbow and label position, then type up to 120 characters in the matching dark text panel and choose Done. Double-click a completed callout to edit its text. Escape, tool changes or focus loss cancel unfinished placement. Snapping honours the magnet and prioritizes nearby path nodes. Previews never export; each completed annotation is one undo step.

Annotations use charcoal 1px lines, open arrowheads and 3.2mm plain-text labels. They are independent measurements of the chosen reference points, not associative constraints on source objects. Move, rotate, resize or duplicate annotations as objects; labels reflect transformed measurements and remain upright after rotation or flipping. They remain in Artwork and cannot become cut lines, sticker outlines, joined paths or node-editable geometry. Delete removes the whole annotation. Include artwork exports annotation lines and TEXT on a separate ANNOTATIONS DXF layer; default cut-only exports omit them.

Node editing

Nodes · N

Node editing sits directly below Select. Click a path to reveal 8px white square nodes; the active node is charcoal. Drag a node to move it locally, using grid snapping when enabled. Active-node tangents use 1px charcoal lines and 7px round handles. Drag a tangent to reshape the curve without moving its node. Smooth nodes keep opposite handles aligned; Alt-drag adjusts one handle independently.

Double-click a stroke to add a node exactly on the curve without changing its geometry. Click a node and press Delete or Backspace to remove it; the neighbouring segments reconnect. If fewer than two nodes remain, remove that contour. Right-click a node for Smooth node, Corner node, Split path or Delete node. Smooth adds aligned tangents; Corner removes tangents. Split creates two independent open paths at an interior node, preserving all curves. Splitting a closed outline opens it at the chosen node. Open endpoints cannot be split. Joined contours can be edited independently; untouched contours remain intact.

Text must be converted to paths first. Local edits turn circles, arcs and polygons into ordinary paths, clearing their parametric controls. Preserve layers, unfilled strokes and exact document coordinates. Each completed drag or action is one undo step. Escape or pointer cancellation restores an unfinished drag. Hidden and locked objects cannot be edited. The dark context menu closes outside or with Escape and supports keyboard navigation. Properties does not open automatically.
